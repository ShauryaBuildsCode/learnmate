# The Water Cycle and Photosynthesis

## Why water moves
Water on Earth is always moving. The sun heats oceans, lakes and rivers, and this energy drives the water cycle. The water cycle has four main stages: evaporation, condensation, precipitation and collection. Understanding these stages explains weather, rivers and why plants can grow.

## Evaporation
Evaporation is the change of liquid water into water vapour, a gas. It happens when the sun warms the surface of oceans, lakes and puddles. Most evaporation, about 86 percent, comes from the oceans. Plants also release water vapour through tiny pores in their leaves. This process is called transpiration.

## Condensation
As water vapour rises, the air gets colder. Cold air cannot hold as much vapour, so the vapour cools and turns back into tiny droplets of liquid water. This change from gas to liquid is condensation. Millions of droplets gather around dust particles and form clouds. Condensation also makes the dew you see on grass in the morning.

## Precipitation
When cloud droplets grow heavy enough, they fall as precipitation. Precipitation can be rain, snow, sleet or hail, depending on the temperature of the air. Snow forms when the air is below freezing, so the droplets become ice crystals before they fall.

## Collection
Precipitation is collected in oceans, lakes and rivers, or it soaks into the ground as groundwater. Water that flows over land toward rivers is called runoff. Collected water eventually evaporates again, and the cycle repeats. The cycle never creates or destroys water; it only moves it between places and states.

## Photosynthesis
Plants use water to make their own food in a process called photosynthesis. Inside the leaves, chloroplasts contain a green pigment called chlorophyll that captures sunlight. The plant combines carbon dioxide from the air with water from the soil. Using light energy, it makes glucose, a sugar, and releases oxygen. The word equation is: carbon dioxide plus water, in the presence of light, makes glucose plus oxygen.

## Why it matters
Photosynthesis is the base of almost every food chain, because the glucose it makes feeds the plant and every animal that eats it. It also produces the oxygen we breathe. The water cycle supplies the water plants need, so the two processes depend on each other.
